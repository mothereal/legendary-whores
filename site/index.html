How to play

Read the gentleman, then try your hand

How it’s done

MP Puts Little Skirts on His Piano Legs. Sharp Girl Takes the Hint.

From our Society correspondent, Mayfair

A gentleman in the District wears his tastes on his calling card and keeps one more under his hat. Lord Percival Plunkett-Bottomsworth, MP, likes Wit and a Mask. Frolic makes him faint. He has also put little skirts on his piano legs. Make a note of that.

You hold a hand of cards: a Saucy Quip, a Peek-a-Boo Fan, an anonymous verse. Play up to three on the gentleman you’re entertaining. Cards that suit him win you Sway; cards he can’t abide cost you. When the Curtain falls, the highest Sway is paid first.

“Madam, I am a Member of Parliament. Kindly be gentle with the Member.”

Lord Plunkett, to the Salon

Between nights, watch him from across the room. Study him once and you learn his Secret Taste. Study him twice and you learn his Kink, which is worth more than all your Wit put together. Spend your takings on the cards and curios that charm him, and build a deck no rival can match.

Your first night takes three taps

  1. Pick a Place.
  2. Tap Best Guess.
  3. Seal it with wax.

The scheming starts on the second.

Strategy

Climb the titles, take a seat, pick a road

The climb

From Dollymop to Grande Horizontale

Our City editor on the price of a reputation

You start Common. Win Renown, the District’s word for fame, and climb to Rare, then Epic. Above Epic there are only seats: two Legendary seats and one Mythic Crown in each era.

High Road titles, with the Whorescore each rung is worth. The Low Road keeps its own: the Richest Tart in Wapping, the Queen of Hog Ranch Row, Off-Strip Royalty.

One Mythic courtesan scores 121. Three Legendary ones score 120. The arithmetic is deliberate: master one era and you outrank a dabbler in all three.

The sporting page

Lady Lavinia ‘Not Amused’ by Newcomer

The District is shared, and so is the Curtain. You choose your Place for the night; so do your rivals. When the Curtain falls, the room is ranked. First takes the lion’s share, second and third take less, and the rest go home with a door gift.

When the arena opens, players hold the seats, not the house, and they change hands by Duel. Challenge whoever holds one: you pick the Place, she picks the gentleman who judges it. Hold a seat to season’s end and your portrait hangs in the Hall of Legendary Whores. Take the Mythic Crown and you hang higher. Gutter Throne holders hang in the Rogues’ Gallery, which has the better parties.

“How quaint. You’ve brought your own enthusiasm.”

Lady Lavinia, on hearing of you

The Boards Opening with the arena

Four leaderboards: Whorescore, Richest, Most Notorious and Most Respectable. Your Whorescore is for life and follows you from season to season.

Your road

Society Pages or Police Gazette?

Two roads to the top. One is lit by chandeliers, the other by a police lantern.

The Society Pages

The High Road

Charm the toffs and your Standing rises. Posh doors open, invitations arrive, and in time a Patron pays you a retainer for the pleasure of your acquaintance. On your portrait, the pearls grow longer.

Seat: the Salon Seat. Board: Most Respectable.

The Police Gazette

The Low Road

Work the gutter and your Notoriety climbs. The money comes faster: back-alley Assignations, black-market novelties, palms greased for a few Coin. The posh doors shut behind you. On your portrait, the blush deepens.

Seat: the Gutter Throne. Boards: Most Notorious, and Richest.

Both roads lead to the Hall of Legendary Whores. One of them goes in by the back door.

Change roads whenever you like. It takes a few nights’ work, not a menu.

What the papers say

Quotes from the game’s own newspapers. The reporters are fictional.

The Colour Supplement

Three Cities, One Street

In the Eternal District, the red-light quarters of three eras share one street. Run a courtesan in each. They never compete with one another, and each era keeps its own clock, its own rivals and its own gentlemen.

London, 1895

From Mayfair to Wapping

“I couldn’t possibly say it, sir. But I could write it down.”

Dolly Mopp, the Parlourmaid Poetess

Gaslight, gilt and gin of uncertain parentage. Work Mrs Featherstonehaugh’s Salon (pronounced Fanshaw), the Tuppenny Palace of Varieties or the Drowned Rat. Entertain an MP, a Pearly King in six thousand buttons and a cracksman who counts the spoons on arrival and on departure.

Lower Bottoms, 1876

Dakota Territory

“I never cheat, sugar. I just know where the cards have been.”

Fanny Faro, the Deadpan Dealer

Swing doors, rotgut and a piano player paid to keep his back to the room. Deal at the Velvet Spur, the Last Chance Saloon or Hog Ranch Row, out by the fort. The railroad baron has never been told no, and would rather like to be. The gunslinger hasn’t bathed since the Gold Rush. The horse thief wears his bandana to eat soup.

Las Vegas

The Strip and Off-Strip, tonight

“Yeah, I know that one. And the other one.”

Jackie Potts, the Jaded Jackpot

Neon, bottle service and a lifeguard who has saved the same man four times this week. Work the Velvet Rope Penthouse, the Neon Flamingo Day Club or Motel Paradiso (weekly rates). The crypto whale asks what things cost, then pays double. The stag do from Leeds sings ‘Suspicious Minds’ to the fruit machines. ‘Slots’ McGee is banned from fourteen casinos and wears his visor indoors, at night.

Legendary Whores is free and open source, under the Apache-2.0 licence.

A comedy strategy game for grown-ups. Every character is an adult and fictional, and everything naughty happens behind a curtain.

No ads, no analytics, no trackers. The only cookie is your sign-in, if you make an account. The typefaces are served by Google Fonts.
