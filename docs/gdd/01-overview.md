# 01 · Overview

## The pitch

**Legendary Whores** is an online strategy game for the phone. You play a working girl in the **Eternal District**, a city where every era's red-light quarter shares one street. This version opens three quarters, called **Timelines**: Victorian London, the Wild West and modern Las Vegas.

Each evening you choose a **Place** to work (a posh salon, a rowdy music hall, a gutter dive), read the **gentleman** hosting it, and play up to three cards on him. Every card that suits him lights up with a green tick. When the **Curtain** falls, everyone at that Place is ranked: first takes the lion's share, second and third take smaller shares, and everyone who came gets a door gift. Between Curtains you never sit idle: quick private **Assignations**, **Rummaging** behind back doors for coin and curious novelties, **Studying** a gentleman from the bar to learn what he won't admit, or hopping to your girl in another Timeline.

You climb from Common to Rare to Epic Whore on fame (**Renown**). Above that sit the **seats**: two Legendary seats and one Mythic Crown per Timeline, held by real players and won by challenging whoever sits in them. Season's end hangs the holders in the **Hall of Legendary Whores**.

## The joke

The whole game is one long seaside postcard. A very proper rulebook (whole numbers, no dice, fair to newcomers) is applied with a perfectly straight face to the business of pleasing gentlemen. The comedy comes from that contrast:

- **Period-correct pomp about rude things.** A Member of Parliament who has put little skirts on his piano legs. A railroad baron who talks about his track ("its length, its gauge"). A crypto whale who calls his accountant "Mummy".
- **Implied, never shown.** The best moments happen behind a curtain: a squeaky wheel, a brass band striking up, a portrait of the Duke of Wellington turning to face the wall. The player supplies the rest, which is funnier and keeps the game on the right side of the line.
- **Mixed temperaments.** Not every girl is saucy. Shy Dolly blushes to the ears and writes verse she'd never read aloud; deadpan Fanny moves one eyebrow; bored Jackie has seen everything twice. A prim character's rare wink lands harder than a saucy one's hundredth.
- **The win pose.** When a girl does well, her portrait shows it: the designer's favourite moment in the prototypes was Dolly clutching her takings after a win. Every starter gets a "pleased with the takings" pose.

## The three Timelines

Each Timeline is its own little world with its own Curtain clock, rivals, seats, three Places, three gentlemen, market cards, novelties, afflictions, gossip and era titles. You run **one girl per Timeline**, so your own girls never compete. All three share one **house layer** (card anatomy, buttons, icons, gestures); each wears its own **era skin** (palette, textures, illustration style, display type).

| | **Victorian London** (1895) | **The Wild West** (Dakota Territory, 1876) | **Modern Las Vegas** (now) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Starter | **Dolly Mopp**, the Parlourmaid Poetess (shy) | **Fanny Faro**, the Deadpan Dealer | **Jackie Potts**, the Jaded Jackpot (bored) |
| Posh / Rowdy / Gutter | Mrs Featherstonehaugh's Salon / the Tuppenny Palace of Varieties / the Drowned Rat, Wapping | the Velvet Spur Parlour House / the Last Chance Saloon / Hog Ranch Row | the Velvet Rope Penthouse / the Neon Flamingo Day Club / Motel Paradiso |
| The gentlemen | an MP, a Pearly King, a cracksman | a railroad baron, an unwashed gunslinger, a horse thief | a crypto whale, a stag do from Leeds, a man banned from fourteen casinos |
| The rival | Lady Lavinia Loosely-Laced (haughty) | Clockwork Clementine, a Temperance League automaton | Brass Bettie, an animatronic showgirl |
| Skin | gilded oil, candlelight, gaslight | sepia dusk, painterly realism, woodtype | neon synthwave, chrome, casino glitz |
| The designer's score | 4/5 | 3/5 | 3/5 |

A newcomer starts with one girl in one Timeline. A telegram invites her into the second after her first Curtain and first Assignation (with a **Later** button), and the third opens when any of her girls reaches Rare. Playing more than one Timeline is optional variety, never a requirement.

## Who it's for

| Player | What they want | What the game gives them |
|---|---|---|
| **The commuter** (five minutes, twice a day) | A laugh and a sense of progress without homework | Three taps a night (Place, Best Guess, seal), door gifts for turning up, a Standing Order that plays for her when she's away, a short gossip-sheet digest on return |
| **The schemer** (plays most evenings, likes to win) | Puzzles with a real payoff | Hidden tastes and kinks to uncover, novelties that exploit them, a rota to plan three Curtains ahead, rivals to predict, seats to take and defend |
| **The collector** (explores everything) | Discoveries, jokes, things to flip over | Long-press any card, gentleman or item to read its back; saucy postcards, gag events, era titles, collectibles like a railway siding named after you |
| **The rogue** (wants to be bad) | A different way to win | The Low Road: gutter money, back alleys, black-market toys, bribes, the Front Page and the Gutter Throne |

It is for adults who enjoy *Carry On*, *Blackadder* and seaside postcards: people who like a double meaning and would be bored by anything explicit. It is aimed at a small first world (about 20 to 30 players in one arena, topped up by clearly labelled computer rivals), which has to feel vast through detail rather than headcount.

## The fun pillars

1. **Read the room.** The fun is in the matchup: this girl, this gentleman, this Place, tonight. The same card can swing by six points depending on who it's aimed at.
2. **Easy to play, rewarding to think.** One button is always good enough; thinking is clearly better (about 1.3 to 1.5 times the fame per evening in the simulation).
3. **Learn by poking.** Every word, card and face can be tapped or flipped to find out more. Players learn as much as they want, when they want.
4. **Never idle, never punished for being away.** Plenty to do between Curtains, but no extra power for grinding. Absence costs little.
5. **Two ways to be famous.** Respectable or notorious, both funny, both able to reach the top, each with its own leaderboard.
6. **Laugh out loud, rarely.** Gags are rare events with a punchline, not a constant wink. Repetition kills a joke faster than anything.
7. **Gets richer as you go.** Day one is simple; tastes, kinks, the Itch, the Roads, rivals and seats arrive one layer at a time.
