How to play

Read the gentleman, then try your hand

How it’s done

MP Puts Little Skirts on His Piano Legs. Sharp Girl Takes the Hint.

Every gentleman tells you what he likes, and keeps a couple of things to himself. Lord Percival Plunkett-Bottomsworth, MP, likes Wit and a Mask, and can’t abide Frolic (it makes him faint). He has also put little skirts on his piano legs.

You hold a hand of cards: a Saucy Quip, a Peek-a-Boo Fan, an Anonymous Verse. Play up to three on the gentleman you’re with. Cards he likes win you Sway; cards he can’t abide cost you. At the end of the night (the Curtain), whoever has the most Sway gets the biggest cut.

“Madam, I am a Member of Parliament. Kindly be gentle with the Member.”

Lord Plunkett, to the Salon

Between nights, watch him from across the room. Study him once and you learn his Secret Taste. Study him twice and you learn his Kink, which is worth more than all your Wit put together. Then spend your takings on the cards and novelties that work on him.

Your first night takes three taps

  1. Pick a Place.
  2. Tap Best Guess.
  3. Seal it.

The scheming starts on the second.

Strategy

Climb the ranks and beat your rivals

The climb

From Dollymop to Grande Horizontale

You start Common. Win Renown (fame, basically) to climb to Rare, then Epic. Above Epic there are only three seats per era: two Legendary and one Mythic Crown.

Society Pages titles, with the Whorescore each rung is worth. The Police Gazette keeps its own: the Richest Tart in Wapping, the Queen of Hog Ranch Row, Off-Strip Royalty.

One Mythic courtesan scores 121. Three Legendary ones score 120. So one era done brilliantly beats three done well.

The sporting page

Lady Lavinia ‘Not Amused’ by Newcomer

You pick where to work each night, and so do your rivals. At the end of the night everyone in the room is ranked by Sway. First takes the biggest cut, second and third take less, and the rest go home with a door gift.

Soon the top seats will change hands by Duel. Challenge whoever holds one: you pick the Place, she picks the gentleman who judges it. Hold a seat to the end of the season and your portrait goes up in the Hall of Legendary Whores.

“How quaint. You’ve brought your own enthusiasm.”

Lady Lavinia, on hearing of you

The Boards

Four leaderboards: Whorescore, Richest, Most Notorious and Most Respectable. Your Whorescore is for life.

Your reputation

Which Paper Will You End Up In?

Behave and you make the Society Pages. Misbehave and you make the Police Gazette.

The Society Pages

Charm the toffs and your Standing rises. Posh doors open, invitations arrive, and in time a Patron pays you a retainer for the pleasure of your acquaintance. On your portrait, the pearls grow longer.

Seat: the Salon Seat. Board: Most Respectable.

The Police Gazette

Work the gutter and your Notoriety climbs. The money comes faster: back-alley Assignations, black-market novelties, palms greased for a few Coin. The posh doors shut behind you. On your portrait, the blush deepens.

Seat: the Gutter Throne. Boards: Most Notorious, and Richest.

Both roads lead to the Hall of Legendary Whores. One of them goes in by the back door.

You’re never stuck. A few nights of good (or bad) behaviour and you’re in the other paper.

What the papers say

Clipped from three eras

The Colour Supplement

Three Cities, One Street

The red-light districts of three eras share one street. You run a girl in each: start in one, and the others send for you as you go. Each has her own gentlemen and rivals.

London, 1895

From Mayfair to Wapping

“I couldn’t possibly say it, sir. But I could write it down.”

Dolly Mopp, the Parlourmaid Poetess

Gaslight, gilt and gin of uncertain parentage. Work Mrs Featherstonehaugh’s Salon (pronounced Fanshaw), the Tuppenny Palace of Varieties or the Drowned Rat. Entertain an MP, a Pearly King in six thousand buttons and a cracksman who counts the spoons on arrival and on departure.

Lower Bottoms, 1876

Dakota Territory

“I never cheat, sugar. I just know where the cards have been.”

Fanny Faro, the Deadpan Dealer

The piano player is paid to keep his back to the room. Deal at the Velvet Spur, the Last Chance Saloon or Hog Ranch Row, out by the fort. The railroad baron has never been told no, and would rather like to be. The gunslinger hasn’t bathed since the Gold Rush. The horse thief wears his bandana to eat soup.

Las Vegas

The Strip and Off-Strip, tonight

“Yeah, I know that one. And the other one.”

Jackie Potts, the Jaded Jackpot

The lifeguard has saved the same man four times this week. Work the Velvet Rope Penthouse, the Neon Flamingo Day Club or Motel Paradiso (weekly rates). The crypto whale asks what things cost, then pays double. The stag do from Leeds sings ‘Suspicious Minds’ to the fruit machines. ‘Slots’ McGee is banned from fourteen casinos and wears his visor indoors, at night.
